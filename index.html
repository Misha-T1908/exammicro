Supply Basics

Supply
Relationship between price and the quantity producers are willing to offer for sale.
Law of supply
A higher price leads to a higher quantity supplied, holding other variables constant.
Quantity supplied
Total units producers are willing to supply at a given price.
Exam line: supply is the whole relationship or curve; quantity supplied is one point on that curve.

Shifts in Supply

Shift right

Lower production costs raise profit at the same selling price, so firms tend to supply more at every price. Subsidies can also encourage production.

Shift left

Higher production costs lower profit at the same selling price, so firms tend to supply less at every price.

Equilibrium, Surplus, and Shortage

Equilibrium
Price and quantity combination where supply equals demand.
Equilibrium price
The only price where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded.
Surplus
Quantity demanded is less than quantity supplied; usually at prices above equilibrium.
Shortage
Quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied; usually at prices below equilibrium.
Efficiency: equilibrium helps scarce resources be used well and possible gains from trade be achieved.

Demand Basics

Demand is the relationship between price and quantity demanded of a good or service. Quantity demanded is the total number of units consumers are willing to purchase at a given price.

Shift in demand means a non-price factor causes a different quantity to be demanded at every price.

Safe distinction: a price change usually means movement along the curve; a non-price factor usually shifts the whole curve.

Four-Step Method

  1. Draw demand and supply before the economic change.
  2. Decide whether the change affects demand, supply, or both.
  3. Decide whether the curve shifts right or left.
  4. Identify the new equilibrium and compare old and new price and quantity.

Circular Flow of Income

Households

Demand goods/services and supply factor services.

Firms

Supply goods/services and demand factor services.

Withdrawals

  • Net saving (S)
  • Net taxes (T)
  • Import expenditure

Injections

  • Investment (I)
  • Government expenditure (G)
  • Export expenditure (X)

Money flows in the opposite direction from real flows.

Consumption and AD

The circular-flow deck states that private consumption expenditure accounts for about 60% of AD.

Factors affecting changes in consumption listed in the deck: wealth effect, inflation expectations, rate of interest, and expectations.
