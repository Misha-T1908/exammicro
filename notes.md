## Supply Basics

Supply is the relationship between the price of a good or service and the quantity producers are willing to offer for sale.

The law of supply says that a higher price leads to a higher quantity supplied, holding other variables constant.

Quantity supplied is the total number of units producers are willing to supply at a given price.

Important distinction:

- Supply = the whole relationship or curve.
- Quantity supplied = one point or amount at a specific price.

## Shifts in Supply

A shift in supply means the quantity supplied changes at every price.

Lower production costs:

- Profit rises at the same selling price.
- Firms are more motivated to produce.
- Supply tends to shift right.

Higher production costs:

- Profit falls at the same selling price.
- Firms tend to produce less.
- Supply tends to shift left.

Subsidy:

- A government payment to firms to encourage production.
- From the firm's perspective it reduces production cost.
- It can shift supply right.

## Equilibrium, Surplus, and Shortage

Equilibrium is the price and quantity combination where supply equals demand.

Equilibrium price is the only price where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded.

Equilibrium quantity is the quantity both supplied and demanded at the equilibrium price.

Surplus or excess supply:

- Quantity demanded is less than quantity supplied.
- Usually occurs at prices higher than equilibrium.
- Price pressure tends downward.

Shortage or excess demand:

- Quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied.
- Usually occurs at prices below equilibrium.
- Price pressure tends upward.

Efficiency means the optimal amount of goods is produced and consumed, minimizing waste.

## Four-Step Method

Use this when an exam question gives a market event.

1. Draw demand and supply before the economic change.
2. Decide whether the change affects demand, supply, or both.
3. Decide whether the curve shifts right or left.
4. Identify the new equilibrium and compare the original and new price and quantity.

## Demand Basics

Demand is the relationship between price and quantity demanded of a good or service.

Quantity demanded is the total number of units consumers are willing to purchase at a given price.

Shift in demand means a change in an economic factor other than price causes a different quantity to be demanded at every price.

Safe distinction:

- A price change usually means movement along the demand curve.
- A non-price factor usually means the whole demand curve shifts.

## Circular Flow of Income

The circular-flow model shows relationships between firms and households through goods/services, factor services, consumption, and factor payments.

Firms:

- Suppliers of goods and services.
- Demanders of factor services.

Households:

- Demanders of goods and services.
- Suppliers of factor services.

Withdrawals:

- Net saving (S).
- Net taxes (T).
- Import expenditure.

Injections:

- Investment (I).
- Government expenditure (G).
- Export expenditure (X).

Money flows in the opposite direction from real flows.

## Consumption and AD

The circular-flow deck says private consumption expenditure accounts for about 60% of AD.

Factors affecting changes in consumption:

- The wealth effect.
- Inflation expectations.
- The rate of interest.
- Expectations.
